# Catch it once, hand it back on time

**The short version.** Neurodivergent adults mostly don't lose memories later. Many details are never stored firmly in the first place, and the hardest part is turning "I said I would" into "I did it, on time." Reminders people set for themselves fade into the background within days. Apps that need daily upkeep get abandoned, and every slip feeds a shame cycle that makes people avoid the task, or the person. The tools therapists trust most (CBT skills, cues placed "at the point of performance," and if-then plans) all work by moving memory out of the head and into the moment it's needed. An app can do that part well; therapy itself still needs a human. No product today combines voice capture, a card about the person you're about to see, quick self-quiz flashcards, picture cues, and your calendar and work tickets. Twinwire's rule, "one habit only: mention it to the app," fits the evidence, provided Altie handles everything else: sorting, scheduling, and pushing the right fact just before it matters, with no streaks, no backlog and no guilt. Connect Calendar and Jira first, because Gmail costs real money to do legally at scale. Keep "Hey Altie" as a wake word rather than always-on listening, and never call the app therapy.

## Eight moves that matter most for Twinwire

| Do this | Because |
|---|---|
| Push a **3-second card** (who, what you last talked about, what you promised) right before you see someone | ADHD memory works much better when an event triggers it than when you must remember at a set time |
| Do the memory work **at capture**: Altie repeats the note back in one line and tags a person, a date and a picture | The weak point is storing the memory, not finding it later |
| Turn every promise into **one "when X, I'll do Y" step** | Planning how and when is harder than remembering the promise |
| Make flashcards **questions first** ("What did you promise Priya?"), with no backlog and no streaks | Quizzing yourself works for ADHD; overdue piles and broken streaks drive people away |
| Let users **pick or snap the picture cue** instead of only auto-generating one | Pictures beat words, and making or choosing beats just looking |
| Connect **Google Calendar first, Jira second, Gmail last** | Reading Gmail needs Google's strictest review plus a yearly paid security audit |
| **Survival mode = capture only**, one next step, no quizzes; the user picks the mode, the app never "detects" it | Burnout strips memory and planning for months; new state laws restrict AI that claims to read mental states |
| **Wake word only, exportable data, never "therapy"** | Legal safety, user trust, and real shutdown risk in this market |

## One in 16 US adults has memory that fails on the way in

This is not a niche problem. An estimated **6.0% of US adults (about 15.5 million)** report a current ADHD diagnosis, and **55.9% of them were diagnosed as adults** ([CDC MMWR 2024](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7340a1.htm)). Worldwide, **6.76% of adults**, roughly **366 million people**, have symptomatic ADHD ([Song et al. 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7916320/)), and a modeled estimate puts autistic US adults at **2.2%** ([WTOP/AP on Dietz et al. 2020](https://wtop.com/health-fitness/2020/05/first-us-study-of-autism-in-adults-estimates-2-2-have-autism-spectrum-disorder/)). The late-diagnosed majority matters for design: these are people who have already built, and abandoned, many coping systems.

The core memory problem sits at intake. A meta-analysis of 38 studies found **moderate working memory deficits that persist into adulthood** ([Alderson et al. 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23688211/)). A second meta-analysis of 19 studies concluded that long-term memory problems in adult ADHD reflect "a learning deficit induced at the stage of encoding," with **no retrieval problems observable** ([Skodzik et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054713510561)). A 2023 experiment shows why: students with ADHD stored less to begin with (0.33 vs 0.44 initial recall), and only **30% reported deep encoding strategies, versus 57% of matched controls** ([Minear et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1186566/full)). The familiar feeling that a conversation "faded after three days" likely describes a memory that never set, though no study has tested a specific three-day window.

The second failure is remembering to act. **Time-based** prospective memory ("send it at 3pm") is reliably impaired in adults with ADHD, while **event-based** memory with a salient cue holds up much better ([PMC3590133](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3590133)). In 45 unmedicated adults, the largest deficit was in **planning** the task; recalling the intention itself showed only "negligible to small" effects ([Fuermaier et al. 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23484020/)). Everyday prospective memory also explains part of the link between ADHD and procrastination ([Springer 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12402-018-0273-x)). A weak sense of time compounds it: reviews find broad timing deficits in ADHD, though adult studies are scarce and mixed ([Mette 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9962130/); [Marx et al. 2021](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/456003)). As one interviewee put it, "my perception of time is as if I want to deliberately forget it" ([Chen et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17258v1)).

Then comes starting. In a 2026 interview study of 22 ADHD adults, many knew exactly what to do and still couldn't begin, running instead on exhausting "crisis-driven productivity." An outside prompt changed that: "If a friend or even the app tells me to go to the library, I can start" ([Chen et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17258v1)). Autistic adults describe a parallel "autistic inertia" in starting, stopping and switching, captured in a participant's phrase "No Way Out Except From External Intervention" ([Buckle et al. 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8314008/)). The flip side is the single most important design warning in the research: **"If it's only me reminding myself, it just becomes invisible after a few days"** ([Chen et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17258v1)).

### Masking and shame turn forgetting into avoidance

At work, **more than 91% of 202 people with ADHD** in a 2026 Simon Fraser University study said they mask their traits, and masking was linked to exhaustion, anxiety and depression, with the extra effort worsening "attention, memory and concentration" ([SFU 2026](https://www.sfu.ca/sfunews/media/media-releases/2026/06/adults-with-adhd-may-pay-high-price-to-mask-traits-and-fit-in--s.html)). Practitioners describe the same daily failures: trying to take notes while following a meeting, an email ping derailing a project, deadlines that seem to "arrive suddenly" ([Simply Psychology](https://www.simplypsychology.org/adhd-problems-at-work.html); [Understood.org](https://www.understood.org/en/articles/common-work-problems-and-causes)). Most workplace tools assume "steady self-regulation and linear time" ([Chen et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17258)).

Socially, the same failure becomes "involuntary ghosting": forgetting to reply, then avoiding the person out of shame ([Inflow](https://getinflow.io/post/adhd-involuntary-ghosting-texting-friends)). About **70% of adults with ADHD** report emotion dysregulation ([BMC Psychiatry 2020](https://bmcpsychiatry.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s12888-020-2442-7)), which turns each lapse into a bigger emotional event. No peer-reviewed study measures reply-forgetting directly, so the forget-shame-avoid loop is inferred, but every first-person account points to it. The missed work follow-up and the unanswered text are one failure: an intention formed during a live conversation never leaves the person's head.

### Burnout removes the very skills a memory tool relies on

Autistic burnout is a defined syndrome of **chronic exhaustion, loss of skills (explicitly including memory and executive function) and reduced tolerance to stimuli, typically lasting three months or more**, driven by masking and demands that outstrip support ([Raymaker et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7313636/)). One participant said "the really, really scary part of burnout is you don't know whether or not you're gonna get those skills back." Recovery came from rest, lower expectations, accommodations and unmasking. "ADHD burnout" has no formal definition yet; the closest evidence ties masking to exhaustion and worse memory ([SFU 2026](https://www.sfu.ca/sfunews/media/media-releases/2026/06/adults-with-adhd-may-pay-high-price-to-mask-traits-and-fit-in--s.html)). The lesson for Twinwire: capacity is not fixed. **Users need the tool most exactly when they can least maintain it.**

## Small lapses compound into a 17% income gap and a shame cycle

Over a lifetime, missed follow-ups add up. Swedish register data show adults with ADHD earn **17% less per year** and spend **12 more days unemployed annually** than peers ([Jangmo et al. 2021](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0247724)). Only **22% of UK autistic adults** held any job in 2020, the lowest of any disability group ([National Autistic Society](https://www.autism.org.uk/what-we-do/news/new-data-on-the-autism-employment-gap)); later estimates of roughly 30–34% conflict across sources ([Autistic Minds](https://autisticminds.org.uk/blog/what-is-the-autism-employment-gap/)). Barkley reports that 86–98% of clinically referred ADHD adults are impaired on daily-life executive function scales, and frames ADHD as a problem of doing what you know at the right time rather than of knowing ([Barkley slides](https://cba-va.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/ADHD-Self-Regulation-and-Executive-Functioning-Russell-A.-Barkley-Ph.D..pdf)). Formal help is thin: a 2022 review of **143 studies found none that directly tested an ADHD workplace intervention** ([Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.893469/full)). Employees hesitate to disclose because they distrust their employers ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/diversity-equity-inclusion/creating-neuroinclusive-workplace.html)), even though 61% of accommodations cost nothing ([JAN](https://askjan.org/publications/Topic-Downloads.cfm?pubid=962628&action=download&pubtype=doc)). A private tool that requires no disclosure fills that hole.

Money follows the same curve. Swedish credit records show adults with ADHD start adulthood with normal credit but default more and more through midlife, and financial distress was associated with about **four times the suicide risk**, a figure reached through a secondary summary rather than the primary paper ([Eton Psychiatrists summary](https://etonpsychiatrists.co.uk/blog/the-neuroeconomics-of-attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-a-comprehensive-analysis-of-financial-behaviour-market-impact-and-systemic-vulnerabilities)). A literature review adds lower savings, exceeded credit limits and impulse buying ([PMC10001631](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10001631/)). The "ADHD tax" is a community phrase, not a measured number ([Saner.ai](https://blog.saner.ai/adhd-tax/)), but bills and renewals mentioned in passing are an obvious target for capture.

Shame ties these barriers together. Among 1,203 adults, those with ADHD reported **lower self-compassion and more perceived criticism**, and low self-compassion partly explained their poorer mental health ([Beaton et al. 2020](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/165039/); [Beaton et al. 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9790285/)). "I know it's ADHD, but I still feel like I should just try harder," one interviewee said ([Chen et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17258v1)). Masking carries measurable costs: reviews tie camouflaging in autistic adults to anxiety, depression and **increased suicide risk** ([J Autism Dev Disord 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10803-026-07557-0); [ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S3050656525000124)), though the evidence is cross-sectional and some non-autistic people report similar costs ([PMC13270291](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13270291/)). Rejection sensitive dysphoria is popular online but is not a DSM diagnosis ([Psychology Today 2026](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/if-i-be-waspish/202604/rejection-sensitivity-dysphoria-the-actual-research)). One small finding points to a gentle lever: the ability to **savor positive experiences weakened the link between ADHD symptoms and rejection sensitivity** ([PMC11859226](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11859226/)). It is a single cross-sectional result, but it suggests a journal that resurfaces good moments with a person before you reach back out.

## Therapists' best tools move memory out of the head and into the moment

Cognitive behavioral therapy has the strongest non-drug evidence for adult ADHD. In a JAMA trial, **53% of CBT patients responded versus 23%** given relaxation and education, and the gains held at 12 months ([Safren et al. 2010](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/632583)). Meta-analyses show a moderate-to-large effect against waitlists (**SMD 0.76**) and a smaller one against active controls (**0.43**) ([Young et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27554190/)), comparable to established behavioral treatments for children ([Knouse et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28504540/)). The UK's NICE guideline sets a minimum non-drug offer of "a structured supportive psychological intervention focused on ADHD, with regular follow-up" ([NICE NG87](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87/chapter/recommendations)), and DBT-based group skills training beat usual care on executive dysfunction in a 121-person trial ([PubMed 36443712](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36443712/)). The gap between waitlist and active-control effects says part of CBT's power comes from structure and steady contact, which an app can partly supply.

The principle running through those skills is Barkley's: externalize information "in the problem setting at the point of performance (where and when this information should be used)" ([Barkley fact sheet](https://www.russellbarkley.org/factsheets/ADHD_EF_and_SR.pdf)). These supports stop working once they're removed, so ongoing scaffolding is the point, not a crutch to wean off ([CHADD 2014](https://www.chadd.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/ATTN_08_14_ExecutiveFunctioning.pdf)). This is Twinwire's theoretical foundation, and it rests on expert consensus rather than direct trials.

Other popular tools have thinner evidence. If-then plans ("if it's 9am and I open my laptop, then I email Sam") showed **d = 0.65** in a 2006 meta-analysis ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/server/api/core/bitstreams/eb48c8cf-7855-478e-90ec-1ea6d44f8252/content)), but a 2024 bias-corrected update shrinks that to **d = 0.15–0.35** ([ID Tips summary](https://idtips.substack.com/p/implementation-intentions-the-science)), and ADHD data comes mostly from children ([Gawrilow & Gollwitzer 2008](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/37368111_Implementation_Intentions_Facilitate_Response_Inhibition_in_Children_with_ADHD)). Body doubling helped 85% of 220 survey respondents by self-report, but a lab EEG study found no significant performance difference and no randomized trials exist ([Wikipedia: Body doubling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_doubling); [ACM 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3663547.3759743)). Coaching studies are mostly small and uncontrolled ([Kubik 2010](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1087054708329960)). Breathwork lowers stress modestly (**g = −0.35**) in general-population trials ([Fincham et al. 2023](https://sussex.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Effect_of_breathwork_on_stress_and_mental_health_a_meta-analysis_of_randomised-controlled_trials/23494097/1)).

Apps can deliver some of this, and adherence is where they break. In adult trials, self-guided CBT-style programs improved quality of life (**d = 0.54**) and symptoms (**d = 0.61**), but users in the 307-person trial started only **about four modules** on average ([PMC13475384](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13475384/); [attexis RCT](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychological-medicine/article/effectiveness-of-attexis-a-digital-intervention-based-on-cognitive-behavioral-therapy-for-adults-with-adhd-a-randomized-controlled-trial/BBB55FF99ADF58005B1BD9B00AD0AF96)), and reviews rate the overall digital evidence as low quality ([BMC Psychiatry 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12888-025-06825-0)).

| Technique | Evidence strength | Can Twinwire do it? |
|---|---|---|
| Externalize memory at the point of performance | Expert consensus, built into CBT | Yes, the core job |
| Break tasks into the next smallest step | Part of CBT; no standalone trial | Yes |
| If-then plans | Moderate in general, weak in adult ADHD | Yes, automatically |
| Breathing prompts for overwhelm | Moderate, general population only | Yes |
| Body-double presence | Weak, mostly self-report | Optional experiment |
| Kind tone and gentle reframes | Weak as an intervention | Tone yes; deep shame work no |
| CBT restructuring, DBT skills | Strong to moderate with a therapist | Reminders and homework only |
| Diagnosis, medication, crisis | Requires clinicians | No; always route to humans |

Read together, the evidence casts the app as **the bridge between sessions**: carrying the structure a therapist or coach sets into every day, at the moment it's needed.

## Quizzing, pictures and event triggers make recall faster

The most reliable way to make a memory stick is to pull it out, not re-read it. A large meta-analysis found **practice tests beat restudying** and every other comparison condition, with summaries reporting effects around g = 0.6–0.7 ([Adesope et al. 2017](https://education.wsu.edu/documents/2018/01/rethinking-use-tests.pdf/)). Crucially, this works for ADHD: in Minear's matched study, "all participants showed a benefit of retrieval practice" (0.52 recall for tested items vs 0.46 restudied), and ADHD did not shrink it ([Minear et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1186566/full)). The same paper's title carries the catch: retrieval "does not compensate for poor encoding" in unmedicated students. Twinwire therefore has to help twice, once when the note goes in and again when it comes back out.

Spacing helps too, and the right gap grows with how long the memory must last: across 839 assessments, the best review interval lengthened as the retention interval did ([Cepeda et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/)). A meeting promise needs a cue tomorrow morning; a colleague's daughter's name needs reviews stretching to weeks. No controlled spaced-repetition trial exists in ADHD, so vendor claims that it "bypasses" working memory deficits are unsupported ([studycardsai](https://studycardsai.com/blog/how-to-learn-and-retain-information-with-adhd)).

Pictures beat words across paradigms and populations (the picture superiority effect), and **drawing a word beats writing it** across seven experiments, because drawing fuses meaning, image and movement ([Wammes et al. 2016](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/a8b52734-18f3-4527-8be2-008a105c85f2/full)). That implies a cue the user picks or snaps should outperform one Altie generates silently; "pick one of three" is a sensible but untested middle path. Context matters as well: recall improves when the original setting is reinstated, even mentally, and most when other cues are weak ([Smith & Vela 2001](https://next.sciencegate.app/document/10.3758/bf03196157)). Combine that with the finding that salient event cues hold up in ADHD and the design writes itself: **trigger memories by events** (a meeting starting, a Jira due date, a name on today's calendar), not by random clock pings.

### Calendar, Jira and Gmail supply the "when" and the "who"

Connected data turns a notebook into a timing engine. Google Calendar's API lists past and future events, supports incremental sync through `syncToken`, and pushes changes through `events.watch` channels that expire and must be renewed ([events.list](https://developers.google.com/calendar/v3/reference/events/list?hl=nl); [push guide](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/guides/push)). Past events answer "when did I last see Priya, and what did we discuss?"; future events with attendee emails tell Altie whose card to prepare. Jira supplies work promises, the issues assigned to you and due soon. Gmail holds the promises made in sent mail, and it is the expensive integration (see the recommendations).

### Faster decisions come from context, not from AI deciding

The research found **no peer-reviewed study of decision paralysis** in ADHD or autistic adults, so promising to "fix" it would be unsupported. What the evidence does support is shrinking what the user must hold in mind. The W3C's cognitive accessibility guidance (COGA) calls for processes that "do not rely on memory," short critical paths and limited interruptions ([W3C COGA](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/)), and interviewees wanted AI that **suggests rather than decides**, with control over tone and frequency ([Chen et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17258v1)). "Decide on the go" should mean one suggested next step with its reason, which the user accepts, edits or skips in a single tap.

### "Journaling made for us" means the journal writes itself

Traditional journals fail the way planners do, leaving a blank page and a dated record of missed days ([AFFiNE blog](https://affine.pro/blog/planner-abandonment-adhd-solutions)). The fix is to make the journal a byproduct. Every "Hey Altie" mention, merged with the day's calendar, becomes a short visual daily record the user never has to write, and can query later ("what did I tell Sam about the trip?"). Paired with the savoring finding, Thriving mode can resurface good moments with a person before the user re-engages, which lowers the shame barrier to replying.

For someone who outsources memory, a shutdown erases part of their mind, so durability is itself a feature.

Neurodivergent users leave these tools for predictable reasons. **Upkeep**: one developer said maintaining Obsidian "felt like a second job" ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/devlinktips/why-i-switched-from-obsidian-a-real-developers-story-and-what-im-using-now-ndn)), an ADHD blogger quit Notion after five or six months of "too many databases" ([ADHD Productivity blog](https://adhd-productivity.pages.dev/blog/notion-vs-obsidian-adhd)), and an interviewee admitted spending "more time organizing the to-do app than doing the task" ([Chen et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17258v1)). **Guilt**: streaks, missed-day banners and overdue badges trigger shame and avoidance ([Life Copilot](https://life-copilot-website.pages.dev/docs/adhd-ux/)), and flashcard backlogs turn overwhelming after a missed week ([Nibble](https://nibble-app.com/blog/anki-review)). **Habituation**: notifications become "overwhelming... or ignorable" ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/nucleusos/adhd-support-app-no-streaks-why-gamification-hurts-more-than-it-helps-1ae0)). **Surveillance**: Friend's subway ads were defaced with "this is surveillance" ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/advertising/ai-startup-friends-new-york-subway-ad-blitz-met-with-vandalism-backlash-10524756)), and an interviewee said of a helpful nudge, "it feels like it's watching me" ([Chen et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17258v1)).

Five gaps stand open. No tool pushes context *before* you meet someone; meeting tools summarize afterward and CRMs make you go look. None ties a voice-captured promise to the next time you see that person. Output everywhere is walls of transcript text, despite the evidence for pictures. Review systems build up debt. And capture is either work-only or one undifferentiated feed. Bee Pro and Omi come closest on capture and integrations, Dex and Mesh on people, Anki and RemNote on self-testing; Twinwire's opening is combining all of them under a single habit. One caution: the research found almost no neurodivergent-specific reviews of Bee, Omi, Plaud or Granola, so their fit for this audience is inferred.

## Build Altie as a friend who remembers, not a manager who tracks

These recommendations follow from the evidence above. Where they rest on inference rather than trials, that is stated.

### Keep the one habit, and make Altie carry everything else

"One habit only: mention it to the app" is Twinwire's strongest idea, because adherence is exactly where digital ADHD tools fail, and setup itself breeds anxiety: "Many apps are too complicated to set up, and I feel more anxious using them" ([Chen et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17258v1)). The rule only works if Altie then sorts, schedules and pushes on its own, honoring the plea "I hope the system comes to me instead of me opening it." Three commitments follow: **zero required upkeep**, **no failure ledger** (no streaks, overdue counts or empty days), and **painless re-entry**, so a user returning after a month sees "here's what matters now," never a backlog. Every nudge should carry an action button, stay until handled and respect a user-set frequency cap ([W3C COGA](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/); [Life Copilot](https://life-copilot-website.pages.dev/docs/adhd-ux/)). To slow the "invisible after a few days" effect, tie nudges to events and phrase them like a friend would, not like a repeating alarm.

### Do the memory work at the moment of capture

Because encoding is the weak link, the seconds after "Hey Altie" matter most. Altie should echo the note back in one plain line ("Got it: deck to Priya by Friday"), attach the person, the date and a life area (Work, Social, Health, Admin), and offer a picture cue. It should then turn the promise into one if-then step ("Friday 9am, when you open your laptop: send Priya the deck"), targeting the planning gap rather than the memory of the promise. All of this must be skippable with one word, and Survival mode should skip it entirely.

### Let the three modes change how much Altie asks, never what it remembers

| | Survival | Steady | Thriving |
|---|---|---|---|
| **When** | User says "I'm in survival mode" or taps a "brain weather" chip | Ordinary weeks | When there's capacity to grow |
| **Altie does** | Captures only; shows answers, no quizzes; one next step; protects top people and commitments; pauses non-essential nudges; offers a breathing prompt | 3-second cards before events; promises become if-then steps; quiz-first cards; follow-up nudges with a ready, low-pressure reply; Calendar and Jira pulls; a visual 48-hour cheat sheet | Auto-built journal and weekly look-back; resurfaces positive moments; long-horizon people cards; goal check-ins; short explainers; export for a therapist or coach |
| **Never** | Adds tasks, quizzes, or shows anything overdue | Shames missed items | Pushes "self-optimization" that rewards masking |

The user should declare the mode, framed as "brain weather," which interviewees found less stigmatizing than overdue lists, with rest presented as care rather than failure ([Chen et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17258v1)). Altie can gently offer a switch but must never claim to *detect* a mental state. Illinois now bars AI from detecting emotions or mental states in therapy services, and Nevada, Utah, Tennessee, Colorado and Maine have passed their own AI mental-health rules ([PMC12677879](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12677879/); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/health-law-and-business/ai-therapy-chatbots-spur-states-to-act-over-patient-safety-fears)). Whether those laws reach a wellness app is unsettled, so assume they might. Memory persists across all three modes, so dropping into Survival loses nothing, and Survival follows the burnout recovery factors: lower expectations, rest and fewer demands.

### Quiz first, picture always, never build a backlog

Users should be able to create a card by voice ("Hey Altie, card: Sam's daughter is Maya, she plays soccer"), and Altie should draft cards automatically from captures. In Steady and Thriving, cards show the question before the answer to use the testing effect; in Survival, they show the answer straight away. Spacing should match the horizon: hours to days for promises, widening weeks for facts about people. Missed cards should quietly expire, merge or re-prioritize instead of piling up, under a daily cap. For visual cues, offer **a photo the user takes, a face from contacts, or one of three generated images**, and test early whether choosing adds friction for ADHD users.

### Connect Calendar now, Jira next, Gmail once it can pay for itself

Calendar is the highest-value, lowest-friction integration. Its scopes are not on Google's restricted list ([Google Cloud Help](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13464325)), though a public app still needs standard verification ([Calendar auth](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/auth)). Jira comes next via OAuth 2.0 (3LO); since **March 2, 2026** Atlassian enforces points-based rate limits from a default pool of 65,000 points per hour, so cache results and query incrementally ([Atlassian](https://developer.atlassian.com/cloud/jira/platform/rate-limiting)). Gmail is the trap. Every content-reading scope, **even `gmail.metadata`**, is restricted, and storing or transmitting that data on servers requires a **security assessment renewed every year**, quoted at roughly **$3,000 to $75,000+** ([Gmail scopes](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/auth/scopes); [Leviathan Security](https://www.leviathansecurity.com/programs/google-casa-cloud-application-security-assessment); [GMass](https://www.gmass.co/blog/google-oauth-verification-security-assessment/); [Nango](https://nango.dev/docs/api-integrations/google-shared/google-security-review)). Unverified apps are typically capped at 100 test users ([Google Developer forum](https://discuss.google.dev/t/how-should-an-early-stage-gmail-client-choose-oauth-flow-to-pass-verification-without-premature-casa-costs/335181)). Run Gmail in testing mode for pilots, then choose between on-device extraction that sends only derived facts to servers (Google has not confirmed this avoids the audit ([forum](https://discuss.google.dev/t/gmail-restricted-scope-is-the-casa-assessment-required-when-mail-is-processed-on-device-and-only-a-derived-subscription-list-reaches-our-server/398372))) and a pre-verified vendor such as Nylas ([Nylas](https://developer.nylas.com/docs/cookbook/use-cases/build/google-oauth-scopes/)). Google's official Gmail and Calendar MCP servers and Atlassian's Rovo MCP speed up prototyping, but Google's are still in preview and the same scope rules apply ([Google Workspace MCP](https://developers.google.com/workspace/guides/configure-mcp-servers); [Scalekit](https://www.scalekit.com/blog/atlassian-mcp-vs-api)).

### Treat privacy and safety as features users can see

The wake word is a legal advantage worth saying out loud. California and about a dozen other states require all-party consent to record conversations ([Layer3 Labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/two-party-consent-states)), and an AI notetaker already faces a class action over it ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/08/15/g-s1-83087/otter-ai-transcription-class-action-lawsuit)). "Hey Altie" captures the user's own dictation, not other people's speech, which is far safer. Onboarding should say it plainly: "Altie only hears what you say after 'Hey Altie.'" If Twinwire ever adds ambient or meeting capture, it should combine Bee's no-raw-audio approach, Limitless's voice-consent mode and the EDPB's call for indicators that are on by default and hard to disable, and never train on bystanders ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/15/amazons-bee-launches-two-new-personal-ai-devices-starting-at-75/); [SmartPlanet/ZDNet](https://www.smartplanet.com/article/limitless-99-ai-wearable-to-promises-to-remember-your-meetings-and-well-everything-else/); [EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-10/20261310_edpb_statement_smart-glasses_en.pdf)). Public wariness runs high, with **72% of US adults** worried about AI-wearable privacy ([YouGov](https://business.yougov.com/content/49367-consumer-attitudes-on-ai-in-wearable-technologies-2024)). Show users what Altie stores, make deletion one tap, keep pattern-based nudges opt-in, and make every memory exportable, so Twinwire can promise what Humane and Limitless could not: your memories outlive us.

On mental health, the line is clear. Call Twinwire a memory and organization tool, never therapy, treatment or an "AI therapist." The APA warns that wellness chatbots "lack scientific evidence and necessary regulations to ensure user safety" ([APA Services](https://www.apaservices.org/practice/business/technology/on-the-horizon/protecting-from-unlicensed-therapy)), and the FDA has authorized no generative-AI mental health device ([PsyReflect](https://psyreflect.com/en/articles/the-regulatory-reckoning-fda-and-states-move-on-ai-therapy-chatbots)). Never tell users they have ADHD, autism or RSD. When explicit crisis language appears, show human crisis resources such as 988 immediately instead of counseling. When Survival mode persists for weeks, suggest a therapist, coach or doctor. And avoid coaching users to "appear normal" at work at their own energy cost, given masking's links to burnout and suicidality.

### Position as neurodivergent-first and useful to everyone

Designing for the hardest case widens the market. Symptomatic adult ADHD alone covers roughly 366 million people, many undiagnosed, and anyone who walks into a meeting blank on the last conversation benefits from a 3-second card. Keep the language label-free ("brain weather," not symptoms) so neurotypical users feel at home. Price inside the **$10–15 per month** band set by Bee Pro, Dex, Tiimo and Reflect, below Omi Pro and Mem Pro at about $29 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/15/amazons-bee-launches-two-new-personal-ai-devices-starting-at-75/); [Omi](https://www.omi.me/); [usagepricing.com](https://usagepricing.com/blueprint/activity/mem-2026-08-25-packaging)). And move now: Limitless owners in the EU and UK already lost service and the rest face an uncertain support window, which leaves a displaced audience looking for a trustworthy home ([Hedy.ai](https://www.hedy.ai/post/meta-acquires-limitless-ai-privacy/); [TechInformed](https://techinformed.com/meta-acquires-limitless-pendant-users-moved-to-free-unlimited-plan/)).

## Conclusion

This research reframes Twinwire's job. Storage is not the hard part; storage is cheap, and wearables already record everything. The hard parts are **timing and trust**: catching a thought the instant it forms, helping it stick, and handing it back at the doorway of the moment that needs it, in a voice that feels like a friend and never keeps score. Twinwire's most defensible advantages are acts of restraint that competitors keep getting wrong: no streaks, no always-on microphone, no therapy claims, no backlog and no lock-in.

The biggest unknown is that no trial has tested an AI "alter ego" memory app with neurodivergent adults, and even good reminders habituate within days. Twinwire should measure its own outcomes from day one with two simple numbers: **promises kept** (captured commitments marked done by their triggering event) and **days to return after a lapse**. If pre-meeting cards raise the first and the no-guilt design shortens the second, Twinwire will hold evidence no competitor has, and a far stronger story for users, therapists and investors alike.
